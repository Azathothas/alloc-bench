   SO WHY NOT JUST DO THIS? Whether it works is a property of YOUR
     PROGRAM, not of the technique: it depends on which libc.a members
     your program drags in, and that changes when you add a call to
     getaddrinfo. An image whose allocator replacement works for one
     binary and silently does not for the next is worse than none.
      The surgery removes the dependence -- musl'\''s members are not
     in the archive at all, so there is nothing for the linker to pick.

=== what this example establishes ===
 musl's allocator can be replaced inside libc.a, and afterwards an
   ordinary `cc -static-pie` produces a binary that contains the
   replacement, does NOT contain musl's, runs a multi-threaded allocation
   stress clean, returns the right answer, and still gets ASLR.
 It does not establish a speed. This repository measures that, and its

   comparison has produced three different orderings on three CPUs
